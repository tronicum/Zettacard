## 1. Why this module, and why Article 5 carries it

The general-staff `dora` module leads with Art. 13(6) as the learner's "why am I here". For a board audience the honest hook is stronger and more specific, and it is **two** provisions, not one:

- **Art. 13 Abs. 6 DORA** names the audience explicitly. Verbatim (DE): *"Finanzunternehmen entwickeln Programme zur Sensibilisierung für IKT-Sicherheit und Schulungen zur digitalen operationalen Resilienz, die im Rahmen ihrer Programme für die Mitarbeiterschulung obligatorisch sind. Diese Programme und Schulungen gelten für alle Beschäftigten und die Geschäftsleitung und sind so komplex, dass sie deren jeweiligem Aufgabenbereich angemessen sind."* EN: *"…as compulsory modules in their staff training schemes. Those programmes and training shall be applicable to all employees and to senior management staff, and shall have a level of complexity commensurate to the remit of their functions."* — **This is not an inference.** Senior management / die Geschäftsleitung is named in the statutory sentence, and the "level of complexity commensurate to the remit of their functions" clause is the textual justification for selling a *separate executive format* rather than one generic course.
- **Art. 5 Abs. 4 DORA** is the provision most executive marketing copy misses, and it is the stronger one for this audience because it binds the *individuals*, not the firm: *"Die Mitglieder des Leitungsorgans des Finanzunternehmens halten ausreichende Kenntnisse und Fähigkeiten aktiv auf dem neuesten Stand — unter anderem indem sie regelmäßig spezielle Schulungen absolvieren — entsprechend den zu managenden IKT-Risiken…"* Two distinct duties sit side by side: an entity-level training duty (13(6)) and a personal currency-of-knowledge duty for board members (5(4)). Questions `dora-exec-haftung-01` and `-02` teach exactly that pairing.

The body of the module is **Art. 5 read paragraph by paragraph**, because Art. 5(2) is effectively a board agenda written by the legislator. Its chapeau gives four verbs binding the management body itself — *"definiert, genehmigt, überwacht und verantwortet"* / *"define, approve, oversee and be responsible for"* — and points (a)–(i) then enumerate: ultimate responsibility (a), data-standards policies (b), roles/responsibilities and governance arrangements (c), resilience strategy **and the ICT risk tolerance level** (d), business continuity policy and response/recovery plans (e), **ICT internal audit plans** (f), **budget, expressly including Art. 13(6) training** (g), the ICT third-party services policy (h), and **corporate-level reporting channels** (i). Point (d) and point (g) are the two that a board most often believes it has delegated and has not.

The other reason this module is worth building: **almost every duty in Art. 5(2) is a duty to approve AND periodically review.** That double verb is what turns board minutes into evidence, and it is the single most auditable thing a supervisor or Big-4 auditor can ask for. Five of the twenty questions test it directly.

## 3. The liability question — read this before changing any wording in the `haftung` topic

The project's existing market research (`claude/dora-cra-b2b-training-roadmap-2026-08-16.md`, distilled from the user's Gemini research) frames module 1A around "liability, Business Judgment Rule" and elsewhere cites "penalties up to 10 % of annual revenue". Checked against primary text, that framing needs three corrections, and the module has been drafted accordingly.

### 3.1 DORA itself imposes **no** personal fine, **no** Berufsverbot, and **no** damages claim on board members

What Art. 50 actually does:

- **Art. 50(3):** *Member States* shall lay down rules establishing appropriate administrative penalties and remedial measures for breaches, which must be "effective, proportionate and dissuasive". DORA sets **no amount and no cap of its own** for financial entities.
- **Art. 50(4):** a **minimum catalogue of powers** Member States must confer: cease-and-desist orders (a); temporary/permanent cessation of practices (b); *"adopt any type of measure, including of pecuniary nature, to ensure that financial entities continue to comply"* (c); access to telecom traffic records under narrow conditions (d); and *"issue public notices, including public statements indicating the identity of the natural or legal person and the nature of the breach"* (e). Note what is **absent** from this list: any management ban / Tätigkeitsuntersagung / Berufsverbot. Contrast e.g. CRD or MiFID, which do contain a temporary-ban power. DORA does not.
- **Art. 50(5)** — the actual basis for anything "personal", quoted verbatim (EN): *"Where paragraph 2, point (c), and paragraph 4 apply to legal persons, Member States shall confer on competent authorities the power to apply the administrative penalties and remedial measures, **subject to the conditions provided for in national law**, to members of the management body, and to other individuals who under national law are responsible for the breach."* DE: *"…so statten die Mitgliedstaaten die zuständigen Behörden mit der Befugnis aus, Mitgliedern des Leitungsorgans sowie anderen natürlichen Personen, die nach nationalem Recht für den Verstoß verantwortlich sind, vorbehaltlich der nach nationalem Recht geltenden Bedingungen verwaltungsrechtliche Sanktionen und Abhilfemaßnahmen aufzuerlegen."*
- **Art. 51(2):** when setting type and level, authorities must weigh intent vs. negligence, materiality/gravity/duration, **"the degree of responsibility of the natural or legal person responsible for the breach"**, financial strength, profits gained, third-party losses, cooperation, and previous breaches.
- **Art. 52:** Member States may substitute criminal penalties; DORA leaves national criminal law untouched.

**Precise formulation the module uses:** DORA **obliges Member States to open the route** to individual addressees; it does not itself impose the sanction. Whether, against whom, and at what level a board member is personally sanctioned is determined by national supervisory, administrative-offence and company law. Saying "DORA macht Sie persönlich haftbar" to a board is **wrong**; saying "DORA verlangt von den Mitgliedstaaten, dass Sie persönlich adressierbar sind — was in Deutschland über KWG/AktG auch passiert" is right. Question `dora-exec-haftung-04` is built on exactly that distinction and its correct answer is deliberately the *"teilweise / partly"* option, with both the overstated ("DORA imposes personal fines and bans") and the understated ("DORA excludes measures against individuals") distractors flagged as wrong.

One genuinely DORA-native personal exposure does exist and should not be undersold: **Art. 50(4)(e) naming of the responsible natural person in a public notice**, reinforced by **Art. 54(1)–(2)**, under which competent authorities publish unappealable penalty decisions including "the identity of the persons responsible". For a board audience the reputational channel is DORA's own, even though the monetary one is not.

### 3.2 The German national-law half (where the real personal exposure sits)

- **§ 25a Abs. 1 Satz 2 KWG:** *"Die Geschäftsleiter sind für die ordnungsgemäße Geschäftsorganisation des Instituts verantwortlich"*, which Satz 3 expressly includes effective procedures for identifying, steering, monitoring and reporting risks. A DORA governance failure will in most cases also be a § 25a KWG organisational failure — that is the hook that matters in practice.
- **§ 47a KWG** ("Besondere Befugnisse nach der Verordnung (EU) 2022/2554", introduced for DORA implementation): BaFin may issue case-by-case orders on DORA breaches — cease the conduct, stop practices temporarily or permanently, ensure continued compliance, take corrective/remedial measures. This mirrors Art. 50(4)(a)–(c) and is addressed to the **institution**, not to individuals.
- **§ 36 Abs. 1 und 2 KWG:** BaFin may warn a Geschäftsleiter, and may demand removal *and* prohibit them from working at institutions in corporate form (the closest German analogue to a "Berufsverbot"). **Important nuance:** the Abs. 2 Satz 1 catalogue of acts whose breach can trigger this enumerates CRR, EMIR, MAR, MiFIR, CSDR, SFTR, BMR, securitisation, GwG etc. — **Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 is not in that enumerated list** in the consolidated text checked. What *is* covered is a breach of "die Bestimmungen dieses Gesetzes" (i.e. § 25a KWG) or of "Anordnungen der Aufsichtsbehörde" — and a § 47a KWG order *is* such an Anordnung. Plus, per Abs. 2 Satz 3, removal/ban additionally requires continuation of the conduct intentionally or recklessly *after* a formal Verwarnung. **So: the German route to a personal activity ban after a DORA breach runs through § 25a/§ 47a/§ 36 KWG, not through DORA, and it is a multi-step route with a warning stage — not an automatic consequence.** *(The § 303 Abs. 1 VAG catalogue for insurers likewise does not enumerate 2022/2554 in the text checked.)*
- **Civil/internal liability:** § 93 Abs. 1 Satz 1 AktG (Sorgfalt eines ordentlichen und gewissenhaften Geschäftsleiters), Satz 2 (the **business judgment rule** — no breach where the member could reasonably assume to be acting on an adequate information basis for the good of the company), Abs. 2 Satz 1 (joint and several liability for damages) and **Abs. 2 Satz 2 (the burden of proof is on the board member)**; for the GmbH, § 43 Abs. 1–2 GmbHG. This is where "persönliche Haftung" is actually located — in national company law, triggered by, but not created by, a DORA breach.

The business-judgment-rule angle the market research wanted is therefore *correct but differently sourced than assumed*: § 93 Abs. 1 Satz 2 AktG protects a board that decided on an adequate information basis — and DORA Art. 5(2)(i), Art. 13(5) and Art. 17(3)(e) are precisely the provisions that manufacture (or, if ignored, destroy) that information basis. That is the honest sales argument, and it is stronger than a scare number.

### 3.3 The "10 % of turnover" figure is not DORA's

Full-text search: **"10 %" does not appear in Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 at all.** The roadmap doc itself attributes the figure to **Romania's Emergency Ordinance 14/2026**, i.e. a national implementing law — the attribution in the roadmap is accurate; the risk is only that it gets repeated as an EU-wide DORA number in module copy. It must not be.

The **only** turnover-linked figure in DORA is **Art. 35 Abs. 6–8**: a **periodic penalty payment (Zwangsgeld)** imposed by the **Lead Overseer**, **on a daily basis**, for **no more than six months** after notification, of **up to 1 % of the average daily worldwide turnover of the preceding business year** — and its addressee is **exclusively a designated critical ICT third-party service provider (CTPP)**. It is not a fine against a financial entity and not against any individual. Question `dora-exec-haftung-05` is built as a myth-buster on exactly this sentence, because a board briefing that repeats a wrong penalty number is the fastest way to lose a Big-4-audited client.

### 3.4 Does this module legitimately address supervisory board members?

Yes, with one caveat worth telling the reviewer. DORA Art. 3 Nr. 30 does not define "Leitungsorgan" itself; it **cross-refers** to the sectoral definitions (MiFID II Art. 4(1)(36), CRD Art. 3(1)(7), UCITS Art. 2(1)(s), CSDR, BMR, MiCA) *"or the equivalent persons who effectively run the entity or have key functions"*. CRD Art. 3(1)(7), read in primary text, defines the management body as the body/bodies *"empowered to set the institution's strategy, objectives and overall direction, **and which oversee and monitor management decision-making**, and include the persons who effectively direct the business"*, with Art. 3(1)(8) separately defining the "management body in its supervisory function". For a German credit institution, the Aufsichtsrat is therefore inside the concept in its supervisory function. **Caveat for counsel:** because the definition is a cross-reference chain, the precise composition differs by entity type (bank vs. insurer vs. investment firm vs. CASP), so per-client scoping is a legal question, not a content question. The module addresses the management body generically and never asserts a German-law-specific board composition.

## 5. What this pilot deliberately does **not** claim

Recorded explicitly so no later scale-up quietly reintroduces them:

1. **"DORA macht Geschäftsleiter persönlich haftbar."** Not supported by DORA's text. See 3.1.
2. **"DORA sieht Berufsverbote vor."** Not supported. No management-ban power exists in Art. 50(4). See 3.1/3.2.
3. **"Bußgelder bis zu 10 % des Jahresumsatzes nach DORA."** Not in DORA — a national (Romanian) implementing figure. See 3.3.
4. **"Das 1 %-Zwangsgeld trifft Finanzunternehmen."** It does not; Art. 35 addresses critical ICT third-party providers only.
5. **A concrete training interval.** Neither Art. 13(6) nor Art. 5(4) names one; `meta.renewal_months` is `null` and `renewal_basis` is `not_specified_in_statute`, matching the existing modules' convention for statutes that are silent.
6. **A fixed minimum resilience budget or audit count.** Art. 5(2)(g) and Art. 6(6) both use appropriateness/proportionality language, not numbers.

1. **Incident-reporting deadlines (Art. 19 + RTS 2024/1772, RTS 2025/301).** Not tested. 3. **TLPT (Art. 26–27) as a board decision.** Not tested. Whether an entity is designated for TLPT, and the board's role in approving scope/budget for it, is real board content but the designation mechanics were not read in full today. Note for later: § 7 Abs. 1g KWG (read) designates BaFin as the Art. 46 competent authority and assigns the **operational** Art. 26/27 tasks to the Deutsche Bundesbank — a nice, verifiable German-specific item for a scale-up question.
4. **Group/consolidated application.** Art. 28(2)/(3) mention individual, sub-consolidated and consolidated levels; a group-parent board's duties across subsidiaries were not worked out and are not tested.
5. **The DORA/NIS2 lex specialis relationship.** Same reasoning as the 2026-08-13 dossier: high board interest, high oversimplification risk, deliberately left out.
6. **Whether German law adds a DORA-specific Bußgeld against natural persons.** The KWG full text was searched for "2022/2554" (29 hits — § 47a, § 60c publication rules, § 7 Abs. 1g competent-authority designation, § 1a/§ 53 scope-extension provisions). If DE/EN ships to, say, an Irish or Luxembourgish entity, question 20's national half needs a jurisdiction-neutral variant. Flagged as a product decision.

## 7. Draft file, schema and wiring
